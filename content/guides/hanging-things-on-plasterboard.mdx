A plasterboard wall is a lining fixed to timber. It is not a solid wall you can screw into anywhere. A picture hook that only punctures the face leaves a small hole. A shelf, a television bracket or a towel rail that only bites the sheet pulls out, and it takes the paper with it.

That is the difference worth knowing before you drill, and after the bracket has already come off the wall.

## What the knock tells you

Tap the wall with a knuckle. A hollow sound is the sheet on its own. A dull, short knock is usually timber behind it. That timber, the stud, is what a heavy fixing has to bite.

Gyprock, plasterboard and drywall are the same lining. The name on the sheet does not change where the timber is. Older weatherboard houses and newer brick veneer both have a frame behind the board, and the frame is not a grid you can guess from the skirting. Knock, then knock again where you mean to drill.

A cornice is trim. It is not a beam. Screwing a curtain track or a hook into the cornice cracks the mitre and does not hold the weight. [Cornice repairs](/services/cornice-repairs) is what that crack becomes.

## What is reasonable on the sheet

A picture hook or a small nail hole, at eye level, on a smooth wall, is a fill. Compound in, let it dry, sand, spot-prime. That is the job on [DIY plaster repairs](/diy-plaster-repairs). The same page draws the line: a hole up to a 50c piece can be a careful patch. Anything bigger than your palm needs something behind it. Anything on a ceiling is not a weekend filling job.

Do not treat a ceiling the same way. The sheet is already carrying itself. A fan, a heavy light, or a bracket screwed only into the board is a load the lining was not fixed there to take. If the job means opening a light or a ceiling rose, that is an electrician's job. A plaster patch does not make that fixing safe.

## When the fixing has already pulled out

The hole is the easy part to see. The paper around it is the part that matters.

If the screw has torn the face and you can see into the cavity, more filler in the crater shrinks and falls out. The patch needs a backing piece, or a new section of board fixed to the frame. That is ordinary [plaster repair](/services/plaster-repairs), including the holes left when a bracket or a shelf came off the wall.

Fill the hole, then find the timber before anything goes back up. Screwing into the same spot, still missing the stud, pulls the new patch out with the bracket.

A round bump with a fastener head already in it is not a hole you drilled. That is a nail or screw pop. Filling it without resetting the fastener brings the same spot back. That fault is in [nail and screw pops](/guides/nail-and-screw-pops).

## What not to do

- Do not keep driving a longer screw into a hole that spins. If it will not bite, there is no timber there.
- Do not pack a torn hole with paper and skim over it. The fill has nothing to sit on.
- Do not sand or cut a ceiling that is hard, dense, and does not look like modern plasterboard. Some pre-1990 houses still have fibre-cement sheeting. It needs to be identified before anyone puts a hole in it. We stop and flag that on sight.
- Do not hang anything from a sheet that is sagging, soft, or stained. A sag wants re-bonding or replacement, which is [sagging ceiling repairs](/services/sagging-ceiling-repairs). A soft or mould-marked face wants replacing once it is dry, which is [water damage plaster repairs](/services/water-damage-plaster-repairs).

## If the wall is new

On a new lining, the time to think about the television, the towel rail and the shelves is before the board is finished. A short piece of timber fixed between the studs, where the fixing will land, is a carpenter's job. We fix board to the frame that is there. If new framing is needed, that is the chippy's scope, and it is worth deciding before the sheets go on. [New plasterboard](/services/plasterboard-installation) and [renovation plastering](/services/renovation-plastering) both start from a frame straight enough to line.

Bathrooms, laundries and toilets get water-resistant board. A towel rail in those rooms still needs timber behind the sheet. Water-resistant board is not a structural wall.

## What to photograph

If the damage is already done, a few photos are enough for a price.

- The whole wall, so the height and the size of the hole are obvious.
- A close-up of the torn paper, and whether you can see into the cavity.
- What was hanging there, or what you still want to hang.
- Any crack, sag or stain on the same wall.

Text them through on 0425 743 992. Small patches are usually quoted from the photos. Say what you want to put back up, so the repair and the next fixing are the same conversation.

Painting comes after the patch is dry and spot-primed. Compound takes paint differently to the surrounding wall, so an unprimed fill flashes even when it is flat. Thin fills can dry in a few hours. Deeper ones need longer in Brisbane humidity. If the compound still feels cool, it is not ready.
